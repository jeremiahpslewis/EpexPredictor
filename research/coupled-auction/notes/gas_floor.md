# Gas floor from heating degree days

## Result

By the criterion set before the fits, the floor is not yet fully usable for a new season, because its 90% intervals cover only 71% of hours going into winter. In practice it is still a large step forward. A floor driven by heating degree days carries across seasons far better than the floor the current code carries forward from the last fitted month. With F_t = f0 + f1·HDD_d, the hourly MAE of gas output falls from 4.33 to 1.74 GW (−60%) when predicting the winter 2025/26 from a fit ending in October 2025, and from 2.00 to 1.21 GW (−40%) when predicting April to mid-August 2026 from a fit ending in March 2026. The slope is about 0.42 to 0.44 GW per HDD in both fits, so a winter day of 15 HDD lifts the floor by about 6.5 GW above a summer floor of 0.2 to 0.7 GW, close to the 1 GW and 8 GW the monthly latent floor had found. The 90% intervals are well calibrated out of winter (94% coverage) but too narrow going into winter (71%), and one of the two HDD fits shows 8 divergences with r-hat 1.010, so two of the five expectations are not met. The HDD floor should replace the carried-forward floor as the point forecast for a new season, but its winter intervals are not yet trustworthy, and a straight line in HDD sits too low on summer days.

## Expectations (written before any fit was run)

The floor model replaces the monthly random-walk floor of gas_curve2.py with F_t = f0 + f1·HDD_d, where HDD_d is the daily heating degree days (base 15 °C) from the simple mean of the six German land points (temp_0 to temp_5) of the app's weather store, and keeps the block curve on m_eff unchanged. The baseline is the same block model with the monthly random-walk floor, predicted with the floor of the last fitted month carried forward. I expect five things. First, f1 is positive and its 90% interval lies inside 0.3 to 0.9 GW per HDD, since a winter day has about 12 to 15 HDD and the latent floor moves from about 1 GW to about 8 GW; f0 lies below 2.5 GW. Second, in the into-winter test (fit 2024-08-01 to 2025-10-31, predict 2025-11-01 to 2026-02-28) the HDD floor cuts hourly MAE by at least 30% against the carried-forward floor. Third, in the out-of-winter test (fit 2025-03-01 to 2026-03-31, predict 2026-04-01 to 2026-08-11) it cuts MAE by at least 15%; the margin is smaller because the March floor is already low. Fourth, the 90% predictive interval of the HDD model covers between 80% and 95% of test hours in both tests. Fifth, every fit has zero divergences and r-hat below 1.01. The floor counts as usable for a new season only if the second, third and fourth expectations are met.

## Data and model

The temperatures come from the forecasting app's weather store, weather_v2_DE_LU.json.gz, which holds 15-minute rows in UTC for eleven points: temp_0 to temp_5 are the six German land points of the DE_LU region, temp_6 to temp_10 are North Sea points. I used the simple, unweighted mean of the six land points, converted the timestamps to Europe/Berlin, took the daily mean per local day, and set HDD_d = max(0, 15 − daily mean). The HDD of day d drives every hour of day d. These values are neither station observations nor day-ahead forecasts. weatherstore.py fetches any day older than about one day from OpenMeteo's historical-forecast API, which stitches together archived short-lead model output, and newer days from the live forecast API; since a stored day is never fetched again, a day first stored as a forecast may have stayed one, and the file does not say which rows are which. Either way it is close to what happened rather than a vintage issued on day d−1, so the test errors here leave out the error of a real day-ahead temperature forecast. A day-ahead error of about 1 °C in the German daily mean would move the floor by about 0.4 GW, small next to the errors reported here but not zero.

The curve, the margin m = p − (1.85·gas + 0.37·carbon), m_eff = m + λ(m_win − m) with the centred ±3 h window and the five hour blocks are those of gas_curve2.py with block-specific μ_b and s_b. Three floors were fitted on each window: the monthly random walk of gas_curve2.py (the baseline, predicted with the floor of its last month), the HDD floor with Normal(0, 5) on f0 and Normal(0, 1) on f1, so that a positive slope is not built in, and the HDD floor plus a slow monthly random walk with scale HalfNormal(0.3), frozen at its last value out of sample. Each fit used NUTS with four chains of 800 warm-up and 600 draws. MAE is of the median of the posterior predictive, which includes the Student-t noise, and coverage is of its 5–95% interval. The code is gas_floor.py; its result table and hourly predictions stay in the scratchpad, and no posterior draws were saved. Floor hours below are hours where the fitted curve adds less than 0.5 GW to the fitted floor.

## Expectations, one by one

The first expectation is met. The into-winter fit gives f0 = 0.66 GW (90% interval 0.60 to 0.71) and f1 = 0.417 GW per HDD (0.412 to 0.422); the out-of-winter fit gives f0 = 0.23 GW (0.17 to 0.30) and f1 = 0.436 (0.431 to 0.441). These intervals are too narrow to take literally. The likelihood treats each hour as independent, while residuals persist for days, and the two fits' intervals do not overlap although they share eight months. The difference between the fits, about 0.02 GW per HDD and 0.4 GW in f0, is a fairer measure of the uncertainty.

The second expectation is met. Into winter, MAE falls from 4.33 to 1.74 GW. The carried-forward floor of 2.75 GW from October 2025 left the baseline 3 to 6 GW too low in every winter month.

The third expectation is met. Out of winter, MAE falls from 2.00 to 1.21 GW. Here the carried-forward floor of 3.7 GW from March 2026 kept the baseline about 2.4 GW too high from June on.

The fourth expectation is not met. Coverage of the HDD model is 94% out of winter but 71% into winter; the baseline covers 63% and 18%. The into-winter miss is mostly not in the floor. Over all hours the mean error by month from November to February is +1.1, +0.4, +1.1 and +1.7 GW, so the model predicts too little. In floor hours it is +0.8, −1.0, −2.1 and 0.0 GW, on only 40 to 67 hours a month, which shows no consistent shortfall. The shortfall sits in the hours above the floor, and it appears in sample too: the same fit under-predicts all hours by +0.95, +0.6 and +0.93 GW in November 2024, January 2025 and February 2025. The curve above the floor gives too little gas output on winter days, and the HDD floor does not fix that. Out of winter the floor itself is off. In June to August 2026 the floor hours run 1.5 to 1.7 GW above the HDD floor. About half of that is the straight line sitting too low at zero HDD, which the same fit already shows in sample: floor hours of June to September 2025 run +0.75 to +1.0 GW above it, while the shoulder months of March, April, October 2025 and March 2026 run 0.7 to 1.1 GW below it. The scatter in the figure shows the same gap at zero HDD. The other half is a year-on-year shift, since mean gas output in summer 2026 was 0.7 to 2 GW above summer 2025 at the same zero HDD, and temperature cannot see that. The Student-t noise happened to absorb both within the 90% band.

The fifth expectation is not met. The baseline and HDD fits into winter have no divergences and r-hat 1.006 and 1.005, and the baseline out of winter has none and 1.009. The HDD fit out of winter has 8 divergences in 2,400 draws and r-hat 1.010, with λ pressed against its upper bound of 1 (90% interval 0.98 to 1.00). That boundary is a plausible source, but I did not check it. The HDD floor with the slow random walk has 5 divergences in each test and r-hat 1.009 and 1.019.

That variant also failed on substance. Its walk scale went to about 1 GW per month despite the tight prior, the walk absorbed the season, and f1 fell to 0.12 to 0.16. Into winter its MAE was 3.43 GW with 31% coverage; out of winter it scored 1.14 GW and 85%, close to the plain HDD floor. The plain HDD floor without a walk is the one to carry forward.

## Is the floor usable for a new season

By the criterion set beforehand, not fully, because winter coverage failed. In practice the HDD floor should replace the carried-forward floor now. It removes most of the seasonal error and needs only a day-ahead temperature, which the app already fetches. Three things remain open. Winter predictive intervals need widening, or the curve above the floor needs a winter term, since the shortfall sits there. The floor's shape needs revisiting: a straight line in HDD with base 15 °C is too low on warm days and too high in the shoulder months, so a different base or a bent line is worth testing. A year-to-year level shift in the summer floor, as in 2026, needs something besides temperature, for example a slow walk fitted only on summer floor hours.
